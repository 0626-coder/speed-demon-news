NHRA Stock Eliminator is sportsman drag racing with a tight rulebook and a wide field. The cars start as factory production vehicles, then get built to a class combination: body, engine family, weight, and a set of allowed modifications. The result is a ladder that can run everything from relatively mild street-appearing cars to purpose-built Stockers that still have to look, in the ways that matter, like the cars they came from.

## Indexes, not a heads-up free-for-all

Most Stock and Super Stock racing is run on an index. Each class has a target elapsed time. Race day is about consistency, reaction time, and knowing whether you can run under the index without breaking out. That is a different kind of pressure than a heads-up Pro Stock pass, and it is why a clean, repeatable engine often beats a peaky one.

Super Stock sits next door, with its own class breakdown and a bit more latitude in the build. Together, Stock and Super Stock are the heart of NHRA sportsman doorslammer racing.

## Why the small-block still shows up

The small-block Chevrolet remains a familiar sight in this world for a reason: parts support, class combinations, and a long record of being built to live on the index. That does not make it the only answer, and it does not make the combination easy. Cylinder heads, camshaft choice, carburetion or injection, ignition, and the way the engine makes power through the sixty-foot all have to serve a number on a timeslip, not just a dyno peak.
